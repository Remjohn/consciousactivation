# Semantic Discernment Architecture Artifact Taxonomy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the full artifact taxonomy for the Semantic Discernment Architecture (`SDA`).

Its job is not to define one more registry in isolation.
Its job is to define:

- what kinds of semantic objects exist in SDA
- which of those objects are canonical ontologies
- which are runtime-derived structures
- which are validator policies
- which are adversarial evaluation assets
- which are execution packets
- and how all of them relate to the existing CCP primitive and archetype stack

This document exists because the current system already contains three powerful but distinct layers:

1. `Perceptual Primitives Architecture` defines how the system detects transformable meaning pressure and forms primitive coalitions
2. `Matrix of Edging` defines how the system identifies and sharpens meaningful tension into executable force
3. `PRD-08 Conscious Primitives` defines the codified transformation operators and coalition geometry that drive quality and resonance

The new `SDA` layer should not flatten those systems.
It should give them a deeper semantic ontology and a stronger validation layer.

The central correction is:

**not all SDA elements are the same kind of artifact.**

Therefore:

- not every SDA element should be a registry item
- not every SDA artifact should be YAML
- not every SDA artifact should be canonical
- and not every SDA artifact should participate in runtime in the same way

Schemas must therefore be derived from role.
Role must never be derived from schema convenience.

## 2. The Core Architectural Claim

SDA is the layer that allows CCP to evaluate not only:

- whether content is coherent
- whether it is resonant
- whether it preserves primitive charge
- whether it fits a strong archetype container

but also:

- what existential pressures it is actually touching
- what symbolic geometry it is organizing
- what directional trajectory it is reinforcing
- what semantic distortions are hiding inside otherwise polished output
- and what longitudinal meaning effects the system is creating over time

SDA therefore sits:

- **under** primitive activation, by defining deeper semantic fields
- **around** coalition and archetype routing, by constraining their admissible meaning forms
- **after** draft formation, by validating directional integrity and hard-negative resistance
- **across time**, by tracking recursive patterns, contextual invariants, and feedback loops

SDA is not a replacement for the Primitive Registry.
It is the missing semantic ontology and validation superstructure that makes the existing CCP architecture more conscious.

### 2.1 Invariant Gravity and Resonance Scaling

SDA should explicitly formalize the observation that some semantic fields carry more human weight than others and therefore scale meaning force differently at runtime.

Three distinct concepts are required:

#### Invariant Gravity

`Invariant Gravity` is a property of the existential invariant itself.

It answers:

- how much human weight this invariant naturally carries
- how strongly it tends to organize memory, identity, and emotion across contexts
- how much latent semantic force is available when the invariant is authentically activated

This is a **canonical ontology metric**, not a runtime verdict.

Examples:

- mortality has high invariant gravity
- belonging has high invariant gravity
- status has high invariant gravity
- convenience has much lower invariant gravity

#### Invariant Activation Intensity

`Invariant Activation Intensity` is a runtime measure of how strongly a specific artifact, signal, or narrative activates a given invariant.

It answers:

- how present the invariant is in this specific content object
- how directly the artifact is touching the pressure field
- whether the invariant is weakly implied, clearly present, or overwhelmingly dominant

This is a **runtime packet metric**, not a canonical ontology field.

#### Invariant Resonance Multiplier

`Invariant Resonance Multiplier` is a runtime measure of how much a currently active invariant amplifies:

- emotional charge
- personal relevance
- identity uptake
- symbolic density
- memory persistence

when combined with:

- the active primitive coalition
- the current edge product
- the active archetypal geometry
- the current representation geometry
- the delivery surface

This is not the same thing as gravity.
Gravity belongs to the invariant itself.
The resonance multiplier belongs to the current composition.

The practical law is:

`semantic force ≈ primitive coalition × archetype fit × trigger pressure × invariant resonance multiplier`

This is not yet literal production math.
It is the correct architectural abstraction for future scoring and evaluation.

## 3. Relationship to Existing CCP Organisms

### 3.1 Relationship to Perceptual Primitives Architecture

`Perceptual Primitives Architecture` already treats primitives as:

- encoded meaning spaces
- perception labs
- transformation operators
- candidate generators

SDA preserves that architecture and clarifies what those operators operate over.

The relationship is:

- `Existential Invariants` define the deepest human pressure field
- `Perceptual Primitives` detect and transform patterns within that field
- `Coalition Signatures` organize the active operator geometry
- `Edge Products` emerge as executable tension objects

So SDA does not compete with Perceptual Primitives.
It grounds them.

### 3.2 Relationship to Matrix of Edging

`Matrix of Edging` defines the architecture of controlled tension selection.

SDA clarifies what the selected tension actually is.

The relationship is:

- `Matrix of Edging` asks: where is the live pressure?
- `SDA` asks: what kind of human pressure is it, how is it encoded, and where does it lead?

In other words:

- Edging selects force
- SDA classifies and validates force

### 3.3 Relationship to PRD-08 Conscious Primitives

`PRD-08` remains the central registry for codified meaning and experience operators.

SDA does not redefine primitives as something else.
It adds the semantic substrate and validator layer that should inform primitive activation in high-stakes meaning work.

The relationship is:

- `Primitive Registry` = transformation operators
- `SDA` = semantic ontology, semantic dynamics, and directional integrity validation

### 3.4 Relationship to PRD-02 CCF Content Factory

`PRD-02` currently routes:

`signal -> coach reaction -> primitive coalition -> archetype container -> JIT script contract -> render blueprint`

SDA refines that into:

`research field -> invariant field -> broad primary signal -> primitive candidate field -> coalition -> edge product -> archetypal geometry check -> content archetype container -> directional integrity validation -> JIT script contract -> render blueprint`

This is the main runtime consequence of the taxonomy defined below.

## 4. Taxonomy Law: Artifact Classes

Every SDA object belongs to one and only one primary class.

The allowed artifact classes are:

1. **Canonical Ontology**
2. **Canonical Structural Grammar**
3. **Runtime-Derived Semantic Form**
4. **Runtime Semantic Dynamics Object**
5. **Validation Policy**
6. **Adversarial Evaluation Asset**
7. **Execution Packet**
8. **Longitudinal Memory Record**
9. **Crosswalk / Mapping Object**

Each class has different responsibilities, governance, persistence rules, and schema expectations.

If an SDA artifact cannot be assigned cleanly to one of these classes, then the architecture is still underspecified and the artifact should not be codified yet.

## 5. Full SDA Artifact Taxonomy

### 5.1 Canonical Ontology

These are the deepest stable semantic units in SDA.
They are not generated at runtime.
They are the slowest-changing objects in the architecture.

#### 5.1.1 Existential Invariant

**Artifact class:** Canonical Ontology  
**Primary role:** define recurring human pressures that persist across civilizations, cultures, eras, and surfaces  
**Answers:** "What deep human tension is active here?"  
**Examples:** belonging, identity, status, sacrifice, mortality, transcendence, betrayal, legacy, desire, fear

**Not the same as:**

- primitive
- archetype
- content format
- emotional tone
- experience mechanic

**Schema obligations:**

- canonical identity
- mechanistic definition
- invariant gravity
- tension poles
- adjacent invariants
- conflict relations
- pressure modes
- distortion modes
- signal indicators
- validator relevance

**Persistence mode:** canonical registry  
**Governance mode:** heavily versioned, slow-change, citation-backed

#### 5.1.2 Representation Geometry

**Artifact class:** Canonical Ontology  
**Primary role:** define how meaning is encoded, weighted, moralized, and directed  
**Answers:** "How is this meaning being represented?"  
**Examples:** earned authority, coercive authority, invited belonging, fear-weighted redemption, status-coded transformation

This is not the same as archetype.
The same invariant mix and the same archetypal geometry can be represented through very different directional encodings.

**Schema obligations:**

- canonical identity
- encoding axes
- authority structure
- fear weighting
- status distribution
- identity framing
- belonging mode
- transcendence mode
- manipulation risks
- drift risks

**Persistence mode:** canonical registry  
**Governance mode:** versioned ontology

### 5.2 Canonical Structural Grammar

These define reusable meaning topologies.
They are not mere labels.
They define how ontology units are arranged into recognizable semantic structures.

#### 5.2.1 Archetypal Geometry

**Artifact class:** Canonical Structural Grammar  
**Primary role:** define stable relational topologies that organize existential invariants into meaning forms  
**Answers:** "How are these invariant tensions being structurally organized?"  
**Examples:** guided initiation, ordeal transformation, witness recognition, moral exposure, redemptive reversal

This should not be reduced to:

- hero
- mentor
- rebel

as loose trope names.

An archetypal geometry must be defined structurally through:

- authority flow
- agency distribution
- sacrifice logic
- transformation direction
- threshold structure
- shadow inversion

**Schema obligations:**

- canonical identity
- mechanistic definition
- invariant bindings
- topology definition
- authority flow
- agency distribution
- transformation pattern
- sacrifice pattern
- shadow inversion
- compatible archetype carriers
- incompatible distortions

**Persistence mode:** canonical registry  
**Governance mode:** versioned structural grammar

#### 5.2.2 Species Composition Grammar

**Artifact class:** Canonical Structural Grammar  
**Primary role:** define how invariant fields, archetypal geometries, and representation geometries combine into valid semantic species  
**Answers:** "What combinations are valid, unstable, or dangerous?"  

This is not itself a registry of content species.
It is the composition law that allows derived species to be built or rejected.

**Schema obligations:**

- admissible combination rules
- forbidden pairings
- instability thresholds
- shadow-drift triggers
- weighting constraints
- validator hooks

**Persistence mode:** canonical policy grammar  
**Governance mode:** versioned but more changeable than ontology

### 5.3 Runtime-Derived Semantic Forms

These are produced from ontology plus grammar plus context.
They should not be mistaken for foundational units.

#### 5.3.1 Content Species

**Artifact class:** Runtime-Derived Semantic Form  
**Primary role:** describe the emergent semantic ecology produced by invariant mix + archetypal geometry + representation geometry + emotional topology  
**Answers:** "What kind of meaning organism is this content becoming?"  
**Examples:** compassionate authority, outsider-recognition satire, ordeal-redemption pressure, witness-based solidarity

Content species should usually be:

- derived
- traceable
- benchmarkable
- revisable

They can be retained in a species library, but that library is derived, not canonical ontology.

**Schema obligations:**

- derived identity
- provenance to inputs
- invariant weights
- geometry bindings
- emotional topology
- stability notes
- shadow drift patterns
- routeability notes

**Persistence mode:** derived library plus benchmark memory  
**Governance mode:** benchmark-informed, moderately changeable

#### 5.3.2 Edge Product

**Artifact class:** Runtime-Derived Semantic Form  
**Primary role:** describe the executable tension object that emerges from coalition composition  
**Answers:** "What is the actual force object we can now deliver?"  
**Examples:** recognition edge, mechanism-reveal edge, authority-sharpening edge, empathy-rotation edge

This artifact already exists conceptually in the current architecture and should remain explicitly distinct from primitive lists.

**Schema obligations:**

- edge type
- pressure summary
- tension boundary
- coalition lineage
- invariant lineage
- routeability profile
- fatality risk

**Persistence mode:** runtime packet plus benchmark history

### 5.4 Runtime Semantic Dynamics Objects

These are not static ontology.
They are runtime-detected or longitudinally inferred phenomena.
They exist because semantic systems evolve over time.

#### 5.4.1 Recursive Pattern

**Artifact class:** Runtime Semantic Dynamics Object  
**Primary role:** track recurring semantic recurrences across outputs, sessions, or transformations  
**Answers:** "What meaning behavior keeps reproducing itself?"  
**Examples:** recurrent shame-to-authority loop, repeated insider-outsider contrast, recurring breakthrough-through-collapse cycle

Recursive patterns should be:

- named
- measurable
- recurrence-aware
- evaluable for constructive vs corrosive effect

**Schema obligations:**

- pattern identity
- recurrence signature
- emergence conditions
- reinforcing contexts
- distortion risks
- interruption strategies

**Persistence mode:** benchmarked runtime library + longitudinal memory

#### 5.4.2 Emergent Contextual Invariant

**Artifact class:** Runtime Semantic Dynamics Object  
**Primary role:** identify constraints that are not universal, but function like invariants inside a specific context field  
**Answers:** "In this coach/tribe/moment, what must remain true for the meaning to stay coherent?"  

Examples:

- a coach's audience only trusts irreverence when it is protective rather than mocking
- a current campaign may require high-authority framing without status humiliation
- a specific tribe may require directness to remain emotionally credible

These are not universal invariants.
They are temporary stabilizers discovered in context.

**Schema obligations:**

- context scope
- invariant claim
- evidence basis
- stability score
- expiry / reevaluation rule
- conflicts with universal invariants or product goals

**Persistence mode:** runtime inference + continuity memory

#### 5.4.3 Feedback Loop

**Artifact class:** Runtime Semantic Dynamics Object  
**Primary role:** model semantic loops where outputs reshape future inputs, responses, and meaning expectations  
**Answers:** "How is the system teaching itself and the user to mean things differently over time?"  

Examples:

- authority content increasing status expectation in future takes
- score-reveal framing reshaping how users narrate failure
- social proof loops reinforcing cultic or healthy belonging structures

**Schema obligations:**

- loop identity
- initiating pattern
- reinforcement mechanism
- visible behavior change
- semantic drift risk
- break conditions
- compounding horizon

**Persistence mode:** longitudinal memory record and analytics layer

### 5.5 Validation Policy

These artifacts define executable standards, not descriptive ontology.

#### 5.5.1 Directional Integrity Policy

**Artifact class:** Validation Policy  
**Primary role:** define what semantic direction must be preserved for a target configuration  
**Answers:** "What does it mean, in this context, for the output to remain directionally coherent?"  

**Schema obligations:**

- policy identity
- target scope
- required preservation checks
- disallowed drifts
- stress tests
- pass thresholds
- escalation actions

**Persistence mode:** canonical validator policy registry

#### 5.5.2 Semantic Discernment Evaluation Policy

**Artifact class:** Validation Policy  
**Primary role:** define how multi-layer semantic evaluation is run across ontology, geometry, species, recursion, and loop effects  
**Answers:** "How do we evaluate the evaluator?"  

This is where recursive discernment becomes formal policy rather than intuition.

**Schema obligations:**

- layer ordering
- required reports
- mutation suites required
- confidence requirements
- re-review thresholds
- fatal failure classes

### 5.6 Adversarial Evaluation Assets

These are benchmark artifacts.
They are not ontology and should not be structured like glossaries.

#### 5.6.1 Hard Negative

**Artifact class:** Adversarial Evaluation Asset  
**Primary role:** represent a deceptively close failure that should be rejected by semantic validation even if it passes surface plausibility checks  
**Answers:** "What dangerous near-neighbor must the validator learn to fail?"  

A hard negative should be modeled as a **contrastive evaluation case**, not a simplistic "bad description."

Its correct structure is:

- target semantic profile
- deceptive near-neighbor
- divergence axes
- expected fail conditions
- mutation suite
- validator expectations

**Schema obligations:**

- case identity
- positive anchor
- negative variant
- divergence axes
- adjacency class
- mutation tests
- expected validator outcomes

**Persistence mode:** benchmark corpus

#### 5.6.2 Mutation Stress Suite

**Artifact class:** Adversarial Evaluation Asset  
**Primary role:** define the transformations under which semantic integrity must still hold or be intentionally re-evaluated  
**Answers:** "What semantic pressure tests should this content survive?"  

Examples:

- compress to hook
- expand to webinar
- convert to CTA
- lower warmth
- raise status intensity
- change audience maturity

**Schema obligations:**

- suite identity
- supported mutations
- invariants that must survive
- acceptable drift bands
- failure thresholds

**Persistence mode:** benchmark corpus and validator dependency

### 5.7 Execution Packets

These are the machine-readable payloads that actually move through the runtime.

#### 5.7.1 Invariant Field Packet

**Artifact class:** Execution Packet  
**Primary role:** capture which existential invariants are currently active, their weights, their activation intensity, and their evidence anchors

#### 5.7.2 Representation Geometry Packet

**Artifact class:** Execution Packet  
**Primary role:** capture how the current meaning is directionally encoded

#### 5.7.3 Archetypal Geometry Packet

**Artifact class:** Execution Packet  
**Primary role:** capture the currently active structural topology

#### 5.7.4 Species Hypothesis Packet

**Artifact class:** Execution Packet  
**Primary role:** capture the derived semantic-species guess before final validation

#### 5.7.5 Directional Integrity Report

**Artifact class:** Execution Packet  
**Primary role:** deliver validator results for semantic direction preservation

#### 5.7.6 Hard Negative Evaluation Report

**Artifact class:** Execution Packet  
**Primary role:** record contrastive failures, mutation failures, and deceptive adjacency risk

### 5.7.7 Runtime Scalar Fields

The following scalar fields should be treated as first-class SDA runtime metrics:

- `invariant_activation_intensity`
- `invariant_resonance_multiplier`
- `symbolic_density`
- `identity_proximity`
- `trajectory_risk`

These are not all attributes of the same artifact.
They are runtime measurements that may appear in:

- `InvariantFieldPacket`
- `SpeciesHypothesisPacket`
- `DirectionalIntegrityReport`
- `HardNegativeEvaluationReport`

The core distinction is:

- `invariant_gravity` belongs to canonical ontology
- `invariant_activation_intensity` belongs to runtime detection
- `invariant_resonance_multiplier` belongs to runtime composition and evaluation

### 5.8 Longitudinal Memory Records

These preserve meaning evolution across time.

#### 5.8.1 Semantic Evolution Record

**Artifact class:** Longitudinal Memory Record  
**Primary role:** track how a coach's semantic trajectory, recursive patterns, and drift risks evolve over time

#### 5.8.2 Loop Pressure History

**Artifact class:** Longitudinal Memory Record  
**Primary role:** store feedback-loop histories and observed reinforcement effects

### 5.9 Crosswalk / Mapping Objects

These keep the architecture connected rather than fragmented.

#### 5.9.1 Primitive-to-Invariant Crosswalk

**Artifact class:** Crosswalk / Mapping Object  
**Primary role:** define which primitive spaces and primitive families are especially sensitive to which existential invariants

#### 5.9.2 Edge-to-Species Crosswalk

**Artifact class:** Crosswalk / Mapping Object  
**Primary role:** define which edge products commonly stabilize into which semantic species

#### 5.9.3 Archetype-to-Geometry Crosswalk

**Artifact class:** Crosswalk / Mapping Object  
**Primary role:** define which content archetypes are valid carriers of which archetypal geometries

## 8. Governance Rules

### 8.1 Role-before-schema rule

No SDA schema may be finalized until the artifact's architectural class and role are explicitly defined.

### 8.2 No false registry rule

If an artifact is primarily:

- benchmark data
- runtime packet
- longitudinal memory
- or validation policy

it must not be forced into the same structural mold as canonical ontology.

### 8.3 Canonical / derived separation rule

Canonical ontology and structural grammar must remain separate from derived species and runtime inferences.

### 8.3A Scalar separation rule

The architecture must not collapse:

- `invariant_gravity`
- `invariant_activation_intensity`
- `invariant_resonance_multiplier`

into one generic "importance" or "strength" field.

These are different measurements:

- gravity = what the invariant is capable of carrying in human meaning space
- activation intensity = how present the invariant is in this artifact
- resonance multiplier = how much that invariant amplifies force in the current composition

### 8.4 Benchmark realism rule

Hard negatives must be modeled as contrastive evaluation cases, not prose descriptions of "badness."

### 8.5 Runtime traceability rule

Every derived SDA packet must preserve lineage back to:

- evidence field
- active invariants
- primitive coalition
- edge product
- selected archetype container

### 8.6 Slow ontology / fast inference rule

Existential Invariants, Archetypal Geometries, and Representation Geometries should change slowly.
Recursive Patterns, Contextual Invariants, Species Hypotheses, and Feedback Loops should be allowed to evolve quickly.

## Appendix A - Theology-to-Architecture Reference Cases

This appendix exists to make the SDA vocabulary operationally intuitive.
It does not make theological truth claims.
It uses theology as a structural meaning system because theology is one of the clearest examples of durable, identity-bearing semantic architecture.

### A.1 Case One - Crucifixion to Resurrection

**Existential Invariant field**

- suffering
- sacrifice
- betrayal
- death
- love
- redemption
- belonging
- transcendence

**Why the force is so high**

These invariants all carry high gravity.
When they are simultaneously activated, the semantic object becomes much more than a dramatic plot.
It becomes identity-bearing and memory-bearing.

**Broad primary signal**

- apparent defeat may conceal deeper victory
- sacrificial love exposes false power
- death may not be final

**Perceptual primitive behavior**

Likely primitive spaces:

- reversal / inversion
- witness logic
- vulnerable specificity
- what-is / what-could-be
- stakes-as-personal-why

**Edge product**

- "apparent defeat is secretly victory"
- "sacrificial love overturns visible power"

**Archetypal geometry**

- redemptive sacrifice
- ordeal transformation
- witness-confirmed renewal

**Representation geometry**

Healthy encoding:

- sacrifice remains love-centered
- transcendence does not erase suffering
- belonging remains invitational

Dangerous encoding:

- pain spectacle
- guilt coercion
- obedience without agency

**Invariant scaling interpretation**

- invariant gravity is high because the active field includes death, sacrifice, belonging, and transcendence
- activation intensity is high because these themes are not peripheral; they are central to the object
- resonance multiplier is high because the coalition, geometry, and representation all stabilize those invariants rather than diluting them

**Directional integrity question**

- does the meaning remain redemptive, or does it drift into domination-through-suffering?

**Hard negative form**

A deceptively close failure would preserve the symbols and emotional solemnity but distort the trajectory into shame capture, submission theater, or authority mystification.

### A.4 What These Cases Teach CCP

All three examples show the same architectural lesson:

- a primitive coalition alone does not explain full force
- an archetype alone does not explain durability
