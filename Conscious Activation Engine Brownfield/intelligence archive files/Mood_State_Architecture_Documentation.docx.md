**SECTION 01**  *— The Discovery — How We Almost Built a Brilliant Broken Engine*

# **The Novelty Problem We Almost Missed**

There is a specific kind of failure that is worse than obvious failure: the failure that looks like success. The CCP's Trigger-First Engine, the Emotional DNA extraction system, the L1/L2/L3 Context Premise — every component we built was scientifically grounded, architecturally rigorous, and capable of producing content of genuine depth. And it would have gradually destroyed the coach's audience engagement anyway.

The reason is a tension baked into human psychology that no amount of emotional precision can override: people do not open social media exclusively to feel their deepest pain. They open it to escape, to be entertained, to feel smart, to perform their identity to their network. A content engine that only speaks from the depth of the coach's authentic trigger architecture — no matter how precisely that architecture has been mapped — becomes a heavy, repetitive monolith that the algorithm stops serving and the audience starts avoiding.

The discovery that unlocked the solution was not a new theoretical framework. It was recognizing that the academic research we had already compiled contained the complete answer — and that we had been reading each paper in isolation rather than as a unified psychological model. Uses and Gratifications Theory (Katz, Blumler & Gurevitch, 1973\) established that the same person uses the same medium for completely different psychological reasons at different moments. Mood Management Theory (Zillmann, 1988\) proved that content selection is driven by non-conscious affective homeostasis — not by quality judgment. Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985\) revealed that three distinct psychological needs drive engagement, and that a system satisfying only one need while neglecting the others trains the audience into disengagement. Together, these theories described not just a novelty problem but an architecture problem: the system had been built to serve one psychological mode of a multi-modal audience.

The practical consequence of this gap was precise and serious. An audience member who opens Instagram at 11pm after an exhausting day at the job they hate is in what Zillmann calls a high-arousal negative state. Mood Management Theory predicts, with empirical reliability, that this person's non-conscious selection system will reject any content that adds emotional heat to an already overloaded state — regardless of its quality, authenticity, or emotional precision. Serving them the most perfectly engineered L3 trigger content at that moment is not a missed opportunity. It is an active negative experience that trains their nervous system to associate the coach's content with additional burden.

The solution required adding an entirely new dimension to the architecture — not replacing what had been built, but wrapping it in a psychological routing system that understands not just what the audience feels but what mode they are currently using media to satisfy. That is the discovery documented in this paper.

**SECTION 02**  *— The Academic Stack — 7 Theories as One Unified Model*

# **The Research Foundation: Why This Is Inevitable, Not Optional**

The seven theoretical frameworks that ground this architecture are not independent validations of separate claims. They form a single unified psychological model when read together — each paper covering a different layer of the same phenomenon: why human beings engage with media the way they do, and what happens when content systems fail to honor that complexity.

## **The Three Layers of the Unified Model**

Layer 1 — Selection Behavior: Uses and Gratifications Theory (Katz, Blumler & Gurevitch, 1973\) and Mood Management Theory (Zillmann, 1988\) explain why and how people select content. UGT establishes the purposive, need-driven nature of selection. MMT establishes that most selection is not conscious — it is non-conscious affective homeostasis. Together they explain the observable behavior: the same person, same platform, radically different content appetite across the same day.

Layer 2 — Need Architecture: Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985), Social Comparison Theory (Festinger, 1954), and Regulatory Focus Theory (Higgins, 1997\) explain what psychological needs each mode of engagement satisfies and what motivational orientation governs receptivity. SDT establishes the three universal needs (Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness) that content must collectively satisfy over time. Social Comparison explains the social mechanics of virality and Status Mode. Regulatory Focus explains why the same insight framed as a gain lands differently than when framed as a loss — and why the mismatch between framing and the audience's current motivational orientation produces friction regardless of content quality.

Layer 3 — Cognitive State Effects: Broaden-and-Build Theory (Fredrickson, 1998\) and Terror Management Theory (Greenberg, Pyszczynski & Solomon, 1986\) explain what cognitive states different content types produce and what the long-term psychological consequences of consistent exposure are. Broaden-and-Build establishes that positive emotions literally expand cognitive scope — making the audience more capable of receiving complex Processing Mode content after Escape Mode content has primed them. Terror Management establishes that the deepest Processing Mode content — content that addresses existential dimensions of the audience's pain — does not merely produce engagement but produces worldview investment, which is categorically different in its loyalty and advocacy consequences.

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Uses & Gratifications | Katz, Blumler, Gurevitch (1973) | Selection | Defines the 4 Mood States |

The operational principle that emerges from reading these seven frameworks as a stack: content that satisfies only one layer of the model will always underperform. The system must simultaneously honor the selection behavior layer (are we serving the right mode?), the need architecture layer (are we satisfying the right psychological need?), and the cognitive state layer (are we sequencing content to produce the right cognitive receptivity?). Failure at any layer produces results the other layers cannot compensate for.

**SECTION 03**  *— The 4 Mood States — Upgraded with Arousal × Valence*

# **The 4 Mood States: From Flat Labels to State Vectors**

The four mood states — Processing, Escape, Discovery, Status — are confirmed by the academic research and should be protected as the foundational behavioral tags of the system. What the research reveals, however, is that treating each mode as a single flat category is an architectural oversimplification that produces routing errors at scale. Mood Management Theory's most important contribution is the discovery that emotional state operates along two independent axes — arousal level and hedonic valence — that must both be specified for content routing to be precise.

## **The Arousal × Valence Upgrade**

Zillmann's four structural dimensions of mood-altering content — excitatory potential, absorption potential, semantic affinity, and hedonic valence — establish that 'Escape Mode' is not one state. It is a minimum of four distinct states depending on where the audience member sits on the arousal and valence axes. A person who is highly stressed and anxious (high arousal, negative valence) needs content that lowers arousal — TTT-02 calm, validating, low-excitation material. A person who is depleted, flat, and numb (low arousal, negative valence) needs content that raises arousal — TTT-04 humor, energy, high-excitation material. Serving the same 'Escape Mode' content to both profiles produces friction for at least one of them.

| Arousal | Valence | State | What Audience Needs | TTT Range |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| HIGH | NEGATIVE | Stressed/Anxious | COOLING — lower activation, calm, relief | TTT-02 / TTT-03 |
| LOW | NEGATIVE | Depleted/Numb | WARMING — raise activation, humor, energy | TTT-04 / TTT-05 |
| HIGH | POSITIVE | Energized/Celebratory | CHANNELING — match energy, celebrate | TTT-04 / TTT-07 |
| LOW | POSITIVE | Content/Satisfied | MAINTENANCE — gentle value, light wisdom | TTT-03 / TTT-06 |

Processing Mode similarly subdivides. The Terror Management research distinguishes between Processing Mode content that delivers an isolated insight (Insight Delivery) and Processing Mode content that is a brick in a coherent alternative worldview (Worldview Construction). Both are Processing Mode. Their behavioral consequences are categorically different: Insight Delivery produces engagement. Worldview Construction produces advocacy, loyalty, and resistance to churn that no algorithm change can undo.

Discovery Mode's academic grounding in Broaden-and-Build Theory adds a critical sequencing constraint: Discovery Mode content performs best when the audience has been primed by positive affect — meaning it lands most effectively after Escape Mode content has broadened cognitive scope. An audience in a narrowed, defensive attentional state will scroll past counter-intuitive Discovery Mode content not because it lacks quality but because their brain is literally less capable of making the associative leap it requires. The medieval sleeping pattern example, the Ford weekend history piece — both require global attentional processing. Global processing is a cognitive state that positive emotion produces.

Status Mode's Social Comparison grounding reveals that it too is not monolithic. Three distinct comparison mechanisms operate within it: Upward Assimilation (inspiration — requires explicit pathway framing or it flips to contrast and produces envy), Downward Active (humor targeting an out-group — drives sharing through superiority signal), and Worldview Validation (confirming existing beliefs with new evidence). The structural design of a Status Mode piece must match its intended comparison mechanism or it will produce the opposite of the intended response.

| HOLISTIC HEALTH COACH — ESCAPE MODE EXAMPLE *A burned-out audience member opens Instagram at 10pm after a day of emotional labor. She is HIGH AROUSAL / NEGATIVE. Serving her the coach's deepest emotional trigger content about 'trusting your body' adds semantic affinity to her primary pain domain — the exact condition Zillmann's research identifies as counterproductive for mood repair. The correct routing: TTT-02 low-excitation content with zero semantic affinity to her active stress domain. Her nervous system needs to cool, not process.* |

**SECTION 04**  *— The 8-Variable Routing Schema — Digitizing Human Psychology*

# **The Psychological Routing System: 8-Variable YAML Schema**

The fundamental architectural problem the 7 academic papers collectively solve is this: human psychological states are complex, multi-dimensional phenomena, but content routing systems are binary query engines. To route content correctly, the complexity of human psychology must be digitized into queryable variables that an orchestrator agent can compute. The 8-variable psychological classification schema is the translation layer between academic theory and executable architecture.

Every framework entry in the framework\_archetype\_mapping.yaml receives 8 new classification fields alongside the existing TTT palette and persuasive angle modifiers. These fields do not replace the existing architecture — they extend it, converting a voice engineering tool into a full psychological routing database.

| psychological\_classification:   mood\_state\_primary: "Escape"           \# Processing / Escape / Discovery / Status   mood\_state\_secondary: "Discovery"       \# Secondary mode served   arousal\_direction: "raises"             \# raises / lowers / maintains   valence\_delivery: "positive"            \# positive / negative / mixed / eudaimonic   regulatory\_frame: "promotion"           \# promotion / prevention / both   sdt\_need\_primary: "competence"          \# autonomy / competence / relatedness / relief   semantic\_affinity\_risk: "low"           \# low / medium / high   sequencing\_dependency: "independent"    \# independent / requires\_positive\_prime   comparison\_type: "downward"             \# upward\_assimilation / downward /                                            \# worldview\_validation / none   tmt\_function: "none"                    \# insight\_delivery / worldview\_construction / none |
| :---- |

## **What Each Field Does**

mood\_state\_primary and secondary define the psychological mode the content serves, using the UGT taxonomy. arousal\_direction operationalizes Zillmann's excitatory potential — the routing engine uses this to match high-arousal content to low-arousal audience states (warming) and low-arousal content to high-arousal states (cooling). valence\_delivery operationalizes hedonic valence — the polarity of the emotional signal the content delivers.

regulatory\_frame is derived from Higgins' Regulatory Focus Theory and determines whether the hook is gain-framed (promotion: 'what becomes possible') or loss-framed (prevention: 'what you're at risk of losing'). The Cesario, Grant & Higgins (2004) 'feeling right' research establishes that frame-orientation fit amplifies perceived content quality — the audience misattributes the regulatory fit sensation to the quality of the message itself. This means regulatory frame matching is not just about receptivity — it is an active quality amplifier.

sdt\_need\_primary tags which of Deci & Ryan's three universal needs the content primarily satisfies: Relatedness (Processing Mode — neural coupling, deep connection), Competence (Discovery Mode — mastery signal, optimal challenge), or Autonomy (Status Mode — identity expression and sharing). Relief is the fourth tag, used for Escape Mode content which does not primarily satisfy a need but relieves need-frustration through deactivation.

semantic\_affinity\_risk is the Semantic Affinity Guard input variable — see Section 5 for full treatment. sequencing\_dependency operationalizes the Broaden-and-Build sequencing constraint: content tagged 'requires\_positive\_prime' must be preceded by Escape or Discovery content in the feed sequence to ensure cognitive scope is broadened before the deeper payload lands. comparison\_type operationalizes Festinger's Social Comparison mechanisms for Status Mode content design. tmt\_function distinguishes between Insight Delivery and Worldview Construction Processing Mode content.

Once all framework entries carry these 8 fields, the orchestrator agent (Alex) can query the routing database with precision: 'Give me all frameworks that serve Escape Mode, lower arousal, positive valence, promotion-framed, with relief as primary need and low semantic affinity risk.' The result is a psychologically precise shortlist for a specific batch slot — not a creative guess.

**SECTION 05**  *— The Semantic Affinity Guard — The Stealth Kill Switch*

# **Semantic Affinity: The Variable That Prevents Active Harm**

Of all the variables in the routing schema, semantic affinity risk is the one with the most immediate operational consequence for audience relationship health. It is also the one that no previous version of the CCP architecture had any mechanism to detect or prevent. This gap meant that the system could produce technically excellent Escape Mode content and simultaneously damage the audience relationship by serving it at the exact moment the audience was trying to escape from the topic that content was about.

Zillmann's concept of semantic affinity describes the degree of thematic similarity between the media content and the circumstances that created the user's current mood. The research finding is unambiguous: high semantic affinity is actively counterproductive for mood repair. A person who is stressed by their failing marriage finds no relief in a romantic comedy that mirrors the dynamics of their relationship. A career professional who is burned out from their corporate job and opens social media to decompress will not be relieved by a brilliantly written, perfectly voice-matched piece about career burnout — even if it is the most empathetic content they have ever consumed. The topic is too close. The semantic field is identical to the pain field they are trying to leave.

## **The Holistic Coach Scenario**

Consider a holistic coach whose core Emotional DNA trigger is 'the wellness industry profits from your confusion.' Her primary audience pain domain is: exhaustion, overwhelm, and the feeling of doing everything right and still feeling broken. Now consider a batch day where the Mood Context Map shows the audience is predominantly in high-arousal negative state — stressed from a particularly difficult week.

Without the Semantic Affinity Guard, the orchestrator might select a high-quality Escape Mode piece framed around the coach's trigger: an observational humor piece titled 'Signs your wellness routine is actually a second job.' The TTT palette is correct (TTT-04 humor), the format is correct (low cognitive demand), the voice is authentic. But the semantic domain is wellness overwhelm — which is the exact pain domain the audience is trying to escape from. The piece will underperform not because it is bad but because it adds semantic load in a domain where the audience's nervous system is already saturated.

The Semantic Affinity Guard prevents this. It operates as a batch-finalization check: before any batch is locked, the guard cross-references the audience's active L3 pain domain (extracted from the Context Premise's Pain Map) against every piece in the batch flagged as Escape Mode. Any Escape Mode piece with medium or high semantic affinity to the primary pain domain gets flagged. The system presents two options: reclassify the piece as Processing Mode (where semantic affinity is appropriate) and replace it with a low-semantic-affinity Escape piece, or apply a domain swap mutation — reframing the hook through an entry domain that is semantically distant from the primary pain field.

| Semantic Affinity Level | Correct Mode | Risk in Wrong Mode | Guard Action |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| LOW — different topic domain | Escape / Discovery / Status | None | Proceed |
| MEDIUM — adjacent topic domain | Status / Discovery | Friction in Escape | Flag for review |
| HIGH — identical pain domain | Processing Mode ONLY | Active negative affect in Escape | Block or mutate |

The Career Coach scenario illustrates the positive application. A career coach whose audience is predominantly mid-level professionals experiencing corporate burnout can serve high-semantic-affinity content in Processing Mode windows (deep L3 trigger material about the institutional mechanisms of burnout) while serving low-semantic-affinity content in Escape Mode windows — humor pieces about unrelated topics, Discovery Mode pieces about historical or scientific facts, Status Mode tier lists about general decision-making quality. The coach's brand occupies both emotional registers without the two colliding.

| GUARD RULE *The Semantic Affinity Guard runs as the final check before every batch is locked. No Escape Mode piece with HIGH semantic affinity to the primary L3 pain domain may be served without human override. The system always provides a low-affinity alternative.* |

**SECTION 06**  *— The Audience Maturity Lifecycle — The Upward Spiral Protocol*

# **The Upward Spiral: How Audience Capacity for Depth Grows Over Time**

Fredrickson and Joiner's 2002 upward spiral research establishes a dynamic that the CCP batch composition engine must honor if it is to serve both audience growth and content depth simultaneously. The core finding: positive emotions and broadened thinking are mutually reinforcing over time. Each iteration of broadened thinking increases the likelihood of positive emotion, which further broadens thinking. Applied to audience development, this means that a new audience member who has only been exposed to Escape and Discovery Mode content has literally built more neurological capacity for Processing Mode content than a new audience member who has received none. Audience capacity for emotional depth is not fixed — it is a trainable variable.

The architectural implication is that batch composition allocation should not be static across all audience cohorts. A fixed 25/30/25/20 split between Processing/Escape/Discovery/Status is a population-level average that actively underserves both new audiences (too much Processing too early, before capacity is built) and loyal audiences (too little Processing too late, when they came specifically for depth).

## **The Three-Cohort Composition Protocol**

| Cohort | Definition | Processing | Escape | Discovery | Status |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| New | 0–4 weeks engagement | 10% | 40% | 30% | 20% |
| Developing | 4–16 weeks engagement | 25% | 35% | 20% | 20% |
| Loyal | 16+ weeks engagement | 50% | 20% | 15% | 15% |

For new audiences, the upward spiral must be deliberately seeded. Escape Mode content (high-excitation positive, low semantic affinity) builds positive affect. Discovery Mode content then leverages the broadened cognitive state that positive affect produces — the audience processes counter-intuitive information they would have scrolled past in a narrowed state. This pairing is not a stylistic choice: it is the Broaden-and-Build mechanism operating as designed. Processing Mode content introduced too early — before positive affect has been consistently built and before cognitive scope has been consistently broadened — lands in a narrowed, defensive state and is rejected.

For developing audiences, the upward spiral is in active operation. The coach can begin introducing Processing Mode content with the confidence that the audience has built the emotional and cognitive infrastructure to receive it. The SDT research (Przybylski et al., 2013\) confirms that digital environments producing consistent need satisfaction (Escape satisfying need-relief, Discovery satisfying competence, Status satisfying autonomy) build genuine intrinsic motivation toward the content source — which means the developing cohort is not merely returning out of habit but out of genuine psychological investment.

For loyal audiences, the TMT research becomes the governing framework. These are people who have transitioned from content consumption to worldview investment — they are defending the coach's alternative worldview as their own. The 50% Processing allocation at this stage is not a reward for loyalty. It is the accurate recognition that these people came for depth and will churn not from too much heaviness but from too little. The Worldview Construction function of Processing Mode content is what the loyal cohort is sustaining their engagement to receive.

Practically, the audience maturity classifier is a simple segmentation layer that Alex reads before setting batch allocation percentages. Engagement depth signals (saves, DMs, comment vulnerability, replay rate, long-form engagement) function as the cohort advancement triggers — not just time-based thresholds. A new follower who demonstrates loyal-cohort behavioral signals within two weeks should receive the developing-cohort allocation immediately, not after four weeks of calendar time.

**SECTION 07**  *— The Trojan Horse — Format-Emotion Collision Design*

# **The Trojan Horse: Engineering Ambush Resonance**

The Format-Emotion Collision is the creative execution of Zillmann's Excitation Transfer mechanism. The scientific foundation: arousal (heart rate, adrenaline, skin conductance) decays slowly after an initial stimulus. When a second stimulus arrives while residual arousal from the first is still present, the brain misattributes the leftover excitation to the second stimulus — intensifying the emotional response. In content design, humor is the ideal first stimulus. It is low-threat, high-arousal, and positive. It lowers defensive activation, broadens attentional scope, and generates residual excitation. When the deep emotional truth arrives while that residual arousal is still active, it lands with amplified force.

This is not merely a creative technique — it is the structural explanation for why the Dopamine Cliff Carousel format outperforms straightforward Processing Mode content with the same emotional payload. The aspiration-building opening slides are not manipulation. They are excitation priming. The 'cliff' is the second stimulus. The audience's nervous system cannot distinguish between the excitation from the aspiration and the excitation from the truth — and the amplified response it generates is the feeling of being genuinely moved by something they didn't see coming.

## **Implementation: Holistic Coach**

Core trigger: 'The wellness industry sells you complexity to profit from your confusion.' The Trojan Horse for Escape Mode delivery: a humor carousel titled 'Every green juice influencer's morning routine, explained.' Opens with high-energy observational comedy about elaborate morning rituals — pure Escape Mode, TTT-04, low semantic affinity risk for high-stress audience. Slides 3-4 introduce the financial mechanism: the margin on a $47 adaptogen powder. The final slide: 'Your body was regulating itself for 200,000 years before the supplement industry existed.' The deep trigger lands in a state of residual excitation from the humor. The audience laughs before they feel — which is the precise sequencing excitation transfer theory prescribes.

## **Implementation: Career Coach**

Core trigger: 'Corporate culture doesn't want you ambitious — it wants you dependent.' Discovery Mode Trojan Horse: 'Ford gave workers weekends off in 1926\. Not out of kindness.' Entry point is pure Discovery — a historical fact that generates competence satisfaction. The hook serves the audience's current cognitive state (broadened from recent positive affect). By the third paragraph, the structural mechanism is revealed: the 40-hour work week was a consumer strategy. The deep trigger — the institutions around you were built to serve systems, not people — arrives after the audience has been rewarded for being curious. They came for an interesting fact. They leave with a reframed worldview.

The Trojan Horse has two mandatory design constraints derived from the research. First, the vehicle (the mood-state-appropriate format) must be genuinely satisfying on its own terms — the humor must be actually funny, the discovery must be genuinely surprising. A bad joke followed by a profound truth is just a bad joke. The excitation transfer mechanism only amplifies a response that exists — it cannot manufacture one from nothing. Second, the payload (the L3 trigger content) must be structurally embedded in the resolution of the vehicle, not appended to it. The truth must feel like the punchline, not the lesson. The moment the format signals 'and now here is the serious part,' the defensive activation re-engages and the payload loses its excitation amplification.

| THE DESIGN TEST *Does the piece work as pure \[mode\]? (Is the humor actually funny? Is the historical fact actually surprising?) If yes: the payload has a vehicle. If no: the vehicle is a pretext and the audience will feel manipulated. Build the vehicle first. Embed the payload second. Never reverse this order.* |

**SECTION 08**  *— Script Prompting Integration — How This Rewires Emilio's Instructions*

# **Script Prompt Architecture: Encoding Psychology into Generation**

The psychological routing system is infrastructure. It classifies frameworks, guards against semantic affinity errors, routes the correct format to the correct audience state, and sequences content for cognitive optimization. None of this produces a single word of actual content. The moment the routing decisions are complete, the system hands a specific prompt brief to Emilio — the script generation agent — and everything described in this document must be operationalized in how that brief is constructed and how Emilio is instructed to use it.

The critical upgrade to Emilio's generation protocol is the addition of a mandatory pre-generation psychological calibration step. Before any script is written, Emilio receives a Psychological Routing Brief containing 6 variables derived directly from the routing schema. This brief is not optional context — it is a structural constraint that governs how the generation process begins.

| \# PSYCHOLOGICAL ROUTING BRIEF — Mandatory Pre-Generation Input mood\_state: "Escape" arousal\_direction: "raises"              \# content must increase activation regulatory\_frame: "promotion"            \# gain-framed hook required semantic\_affinity\_risk: "low"            \# topic must NOT mirror primary pain domain sequencing\_dependency: "independent"     \# no positive prime required upstream payload\_masking\_instruction: |   You are writing in ESCAPE / DISCOVERY collision mode.   The L3 payload must arrive through the vehicle's natural resolution.   The audience must feel smart or entertained BEFORE they feel seen.   The vehicle must work as pure \[mode\] independent of the payload.   Never signal the transition from vehicle to payload.   The truth is the punchline. Not the lesson. |
| :---- |

The payload\_masking\_instruction is the operationalization of the Trojan Horse design principle for each specific mood state. For Processing Mode, there is no masking — the payload is the content. For Escape Mode, the instruction specifies that the core L3 truth must arrive through humor or absurdity as subtext, never as subject. For Discovery Mode, the instruction specifies that the L3 payload must be embedded in the resolution of a genuinely surprising fact or counter-intuitive claim. For Status Mode, the instruction specifies that the truth must be encoded in the comparison mechanism — in what the tier list rankings reveal, not in an explicit lesson.

The Regulatory Frame variable governs hook engineering. Regulatory Focus Theory's Lee & Aaker (2004) research proves that gain-framed messages outperform loss-framed messages with promotion-focused audiences, and vice versa, even when the underlying information content is identical. Emilio's hook generation must honor this: when the routing brief specifies promotion frame, the hook must lead with what becomes possible, not what is at risk. When it specifies prevention frame, the hook must lead with what the audience stands to lose if they don't act.

The arousal\_direction variable governs pacing and energy engineering. When the brief specifies 'raises' (warming a depleted audience), sentence rhythm should shorten, certainty markers should increase, humor should be high-energy rather than dry. When the brief specifies 'lowers' (cooling a stressed audience), rhythm should lengthen, empathy markers should increase, the opening should validate before it challenges. The Voice DNA's TTT modulation rules already encode these patterns — the Psychological Routing Brief simply activates the correct modulation before generation begins rather than leaving it to post-hoc revision.

The complete generation flow for a single script is therefore: Trigger Matching Layer produces the Activation Event Seed → Routing Engine queries the psychological classification schema to select the optimal framework → Routing Engine assembles the Psychological Routing Brief → Brief is injected into Emilio's prompt alongside the standard 8-input contract (Voice DNA SPR, Context Premise, Story Formula, etc.) → Emilio executes generation with the psychological calibration active from the first sentence.

**SECTION 09**  *— Skill Authoring Integration — And the Case for a Separate Scripts Guide*

# **Skill Architecture Integration: What Changes and What Gets Built**

The Skill Authoring Guide V4 is already architecturally sound. The principles are correct, the directives are rigorous, and the ontological separation of Agent, Subagent, and Skill is well-enforced. What the Psychological Routing Architecture adds is not a reason to rebuild the guide — it is a reason to extend it with a new classification layer and a specialized sub-guide for the specific domain of script-generating skills.

## **Changes to the Existing YAML Frontmatter Standard**

Every CCF script-generating skill (the SoC Generator, the Script Generator, the Mirror Session, the Wisdom Forge) needs two new mandatory frontmatter fields added to the V4 standard:

| \# New V4.1 fields for CCF script-generating skills psychological\_routing:   accepts\_routing\_brief: true         \# skill ingests Psychological Routing Brief   mood\_states\_supported:              \# which mood states this skill can generate for     \- Processing     \- Escape     \- Discovery     \- Status   payload\_masking\_capable: true       \# skill supports Trojan Horse instruction   regulatory\_frame\_sensitive: true    \# skill honors promotion/prevention framing |
| :---- |

The accepts\_routing\_brief field signals to the Orchestrator that this skill requires the Psychological Routing Brief as a mandatory input — it cannot run without it. This enforces the routing architecture at the dependency level rather than relying on prompt instructions to be honored. The mood\_states\_supported field is the routing lookup that Alex queries to confirm a skill can produce content for the target mood state before invoking it.

## **Changes to the Cognitive State Instruction Standard**

The V4 Cognitive State Instruction currently specifies the operation to perform: 'Execute a compression operation.' For script-generating skills, the Cognitive State Instruction must be extended to include the psychological mode activation as a pre-condition: 'The Psychological Routing Brief is the first constraint this generation must honor. Read and internalize all 6 routing variables before activating the construction mechanics.' This extension does not change the instruction's structure — it prepends a mandatory calibration step that the generation cannot proceed without acknowledging.

## **Should We Build a Separate Scripts Skill Authoring Guide?**

Yes. And here is precisely why, grounded in the architecture paper's own logic.

The CCP Skill Architecture Paper establishes that SteerEval's research shows LLM behavioral control degrades at finer-grained levels — dropping from 2.76 at L1 (intent) to 0.07 at L3 (implementation). Script-generating skills operate at L3. They are the most granular, most controllability-sensitive skills in the entire pipeline. The generic Skill Authoring Guide V4 was designed to cover 65+ skills across 5 sub-systems. Asking it to also encode the highly specific psychological routing requirements of script generation is asking it to span a specificity range it was not designed for.

A dedicated Scripts Skill Authoring Guide — the CCF Script Generation Skill Standard — would cover: mandatory Psychological Routing Brief schema and ingestion protocol, the 5 payload masking instruction templates (one per mood state plus neutral), regulatory frame hook engineering rules derived from Lee & Aaker (2004), arousal direction pacing protocols derived from Zillmann's excitation potential research, the Semantic Affinity Guard contract for script-generating skills, Format-Emotion Collision design constraints, and the Audience Maturity modifier that adjusts Processing Mode depth based on cohort classification.

The guide would not replace the Skill Authoring Guide V4 — it would extend it, in the same way V4 extended V3. All V4 directives remain in force. The Scripts guide adds a psychological routing layer on top that is specific to the CCF content generation domain. It becomes the mandatory companion document for any engineer authoring or revising a script-generating skill within the CCF pipeline. Skills outside the CCF pipeline — CMF, CBCS, V2WS — do not require it.

The Conscious Composable Skill Builder (CCSB) should recognize the Scripts guide as a domain-specific authoring standard during Phase 1 Design Brief creation. When the CCSB identifies that a proposed skill falls within the CCF script generation domain, the Design Brief should automatically flag the Scripts guide as a required reference alongside the main Skill Authoring Guide V4. This prevents the most common authoring failure: a new skill engineered to V4 standard that nonetheless produces psychologically miscalibrated output because it never received the routing architecture's constraints.
