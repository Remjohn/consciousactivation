# Phase 6 Definition Grammar Protocols

## 1. PrimitiveCandidate Grammar

A PrimitiveCandidate definition MUST state:
1. which canonical PrimitiveDefinition it instantiates;
2. what source evidence activates it;
3. what transformation it proposes;
4. what semantic substrate it operates on;
5. what expected directional effect it creates;
6. what evidence would falsify or weaken the candidate;
7. what admissible range applies.

Target definition length: 100–180 words.

## 2. CandidateAssessment Grammar

Must distinguish:
- evidence facts
- inferred properties
- evaluator scores
- unresolved uncertainty.

Never collapse confidence into truth.

## 3. CoalitionCandidate Grammar

Must state:
- candidate members,
- intended interaction,
- compatibility rationale,
- expected emergent force,
- routeability hypothesis,
- known fatality risks.

Target: 120–220 words.

## 4. CoalitionSignature Grammar

Must describe the selected sparse operator geometry:
- members
- weights
- ordering
- interaction modes
- family saturation
- admissibility
- route.

It is a decision artifact, not a prose essay.

## 5. EdgeProduct Grammar

Must answer:
- what tension boundary is being activated;
- what expectation, contradiction, asymmetry, or recognition pressure produces it;
- what coalition created it;
- what invariant and audience/Guest structures support it;
- how it differs from adjacent edges;
- what route can express it.

An Edge Product MUST NOT be defined merely as a “hook,” “angle,” “idea,” “theme,” or “message.”

## 6. Anti-centroid definition rule

Definitions MUST preserve specific tension. They must not introduce generic caveats that weaken the object’s force or turn unusual meaning into a prohibited category merely because it is unusual.

## 7. Evidence rule

Every derived semantic definition MUST cite its upstream evidence and distinguish observation from interpretation.
