# Pressure & Edging Constitutional Principles

## Principle 1 — Pressure is selected, not invented
The engine may identify pressure present in the modeled evidence. It may not manufacture a dramatic pressure merely because it would produce a stronger-looking activation.

## Principle 2 — Broad before sharp
The pre-interview signal must be broad enough to preserve Guest agency. Sharp conclusions belong downstream of authenticated evidence.

## Principle 3 — Tension over topic
Topic relevance alone cannot make a candidate eligible. The candidate must identify a meaningful unresolved boundary.

## Principle 4 — Evidence before certainty
A high score may prioritize investigation; it may not convert weak evidence into fact.

## Principle 5 — Collision is signal
Contradictions, asymmetries, anomalies, shadows, and unarticulated regularities must remain visible when they are supported.

## Principle 6 — Anti-centroid is not anti-sharpness
A candidate is not unsafe merely because it is uncomfortable, controversial, emotionally strong, or difficult to reconcile. The question is whether it is grounded and executable.

## Principle 7 — No imported censorship
The system must not introduce generic cautionary language, authorization clauses, moralizing disclaimers, or audience-softening language unless a canonical policy specifically requires it for the current task.

## Principle 8 — Matrix of Edging is a force-selection doctrine
It governs tension selection and pressure concentration. It does not replace the SFL, archetype, scene, or media realization layers.

## Principle 9 — Saturation matters
Repeated firing without new evidence must trigger saturation controls. Repetition is not automatically fresh signal.

## Principle 10 — Human truth is earned
No upstream candidate can become Guest truth before human response and evidence validation.
