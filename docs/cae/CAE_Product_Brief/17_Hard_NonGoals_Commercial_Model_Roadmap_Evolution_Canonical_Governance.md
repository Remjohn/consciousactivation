# 17. Hard Non-Goals, Commercial Model, Roadmap, Evolution & Canonical Governance

CAE requires a strong ending because the product can technically do far more than it should productize. The repository contains broad intelligence, media, orchestration, and application capabilities, but capability is not the same thing as product scope. The canonical Product Brief must therefore define not only what CAE does but what it refuses to become. These non-goals are constitutional boundaries, not deferred features.

The first hard non-goal is **no real-time AI intervention in the live human interaction**. CAE may prepare research, questions, elicitation guidance, and post-interview analysis, but the product's declared production model does not require an AI copilot whispering interventions into the Operator's live conversation. This protects the human relationship, avoids unnecessary latency dependence, and keeps the interview as a deliberate evidence-generation event rather than a model-mediated performance.

The second is **no self-service client portal as the primary product model**. The Leader or Subject is the source of evidence, but the Operator is the primary engine user. A self-service portal could eventually become an adapter or limited review surface, but it should not redefine the product as a generic SaaS workspace where clients independently prompt and publish. The Operator-conduit architecture is central to both quality and commercial differentiation.

The third is **no synthetic Subject dialogue**. CAE must never manufacture words and present them as though the Leader said them. The system may support governed editorial bridges where the composition contract explicitly permits them, but the product will not use voice cloning or generated dialogue to make a human appear to speak invented material. This prohibition protects the central promise of evidence sovereignty.

The fourth is **no automated psychological profiling from arbitrary data**. The Subject Constitution must be built through legitimate elicitation and human confirmation, not by silently inferring deep identity or psychological properties from random social content. This boundary prevents a convenient machine inference from becoming a false canonical description of a person.

The fifth is **no post-hoc artifact discovery**. CAE does not record a general interview and then invent the production requirements afterward. Artifact requirements, narrative objectives, and evidence needs are declared before the session so the Operator can conduct a purposeful interview. This is one of the main ways CAE avoids the evidence-loss problem described earlier in the Product Brief.

The sixth is **no post-hoc format squeezing**. A piece of evidence that only works as a conversation cannot be forced into a carousel because the quota requires another carousel. Format determines elicitation and composition requirements. When evidence does not support the format, the system should find another legitimate route or report the shortfall.

The seventh is **no automated external distribution as a constitutional CAE responsibility**. Release preparation, manifests, audit exports, and handoff can be governed by CAE. Direct publishing to external platforms may be supported through controlled integrations in the future, but automated distribution must not become the hidden endpoint of the product. The release boundary remains explicit.

The eighth is **no generative impersonation**. This broadens the dialogue prohibition to cover synthetic performances that materially misrepresent the Subject. CAE can render media and may use approved generated support assets within their contract, but it should not manufacture an identity performance that viewers reasonably believe is authentic Subject expression.

Commercially, the product is built around the Operator's ability to serve a portfolio of Leaders. The original economic insight was that high-quality interview-driven personal branding was historically expensive and difficult to scale without quality degradation. CAE's commercial model therefore values **production leverage**, not software-seat volume. Retainer production is the natural core: predictable recurring packages where the Operator can plan interviews, maintain a persistent Subject Constitution, produce a defined artifact envelope, and use accumulated evidence to improve future cycles.

An Opportunist mode can complement the retainer by allowing timely production around a news event, cultural moment, newly surfaced insight, or collision. Its commercial value comes from speed and relevance. It should not become an excuse to bypass evidence or authority. The pricing or internal budget can differ from a retainer, but the constitutional contracts remain the same.

The roadmap should be described as a **completion and convergence roadmap**, not a promise to build every conceivable idea. The first priority is to converge the current architecture around a stable production spine: canonical contracts, runtime state, Subject and interview flow, evidence sovereignty, composition lanes, Operator control surface, release/ship, observability, and outcomes. The next priority is certification and intelligence economics: CSEB, model routing, task-specific certification, cost telemetry, and post-training datasets. Later work can expand media intelligence, new production modes, richer adapters, and additional artifact forms without reopening the foundational constitution each time.

Product evolution should follow a strict rule: **extend contracts; do not bypass them**. New models should enter through certification. New formats should declare a composition lane. New campaign modes should declare their lifecycle and output contract. New integrations should operate through the canonical runtime. New learning loops should preserve the distinction between evidence, inference, human correction, and outcome. New automation should be evaluated against the same non-goals rather than being allowed to create exceptions merely because it is technically possible.

Canonical governance therefore requires an explicit amendment process. The Product Brief defines the identity, target users, core problem, capability pillars, authority model, non-goals, success metrics, and customer-facing guarantees. A change that only alters implementation should remain in the repository's lower-level authorities. A change that alters product identity or constitutional boundaries should trigger Product Brief review before implementation is treated as canonical. The hierarchy remains:

**Product Brief → Product Requirements → Governance / Constitutions → Schemas & Contracts → Services / Programs → Tests & Acceptance Evidence.**

This hierarchy prevents two opposite errors. The first is allowing implementation to redefine the product merely because code happened to grow a capability. The second is writing a product promise that the implementation cannot enforce. Product decisions establish intent; implementation artifacts establish enforceability; tests and acceptance evidence prove whether the intended contract is actually realized.

Amendments should be versioned and reasoned. The record should identify the proposed change, why the current contract is insufficient, which downstream contracts are affected, what new failure modes appear, what evidence supports the change, and whether the change alters commercial positioning or customer guarantees. The amendment should be accepted only after the corresponding repository authorities and tests are updated or a clear migration plan exists.

The roadmap should also preserve historical truth. CAE's architecture has evolved from an earlier product brief that did not fully capture the current breadth of interviews, tenancy, media intelligence, outcome learning, and operator control. The workspace README explicitly notes that some constitutional amendments may be represented structurally without every amendment already being implemented in live artifacts. The final evolution principle is consequently:

> **CAE may become more intelligent, more economical, more multimodal, and more operationally autonomous; it must not become less sovereign over evidence, less explicit about authority, or more willing to fabricate what the human source did not provide.**

That principle gives the product room to evolve for years without losing its identity. Models will change. Interfaces will change. Retrieval providers will change. Media sources will change. Campaign modes will grow. Post-training may reduce dependence on expensive frontier inference. None of that should change what CAE is.

CAE is an Operator-conduit, evidence-sovereign activation production system. It exists to turn differentiated human knowledge into governed public activation at economically useful scale. Its commercial value comes from leverage. Its technical value comes from the causal chain and the intelligence architecture. Its trustworthiness comes from explicit authority and fail-closed behavior. Its moat comes from accumulated evidence, production telemetry, benchmark knowledge, and human correction data.

The final canonical boundary is therefore both positive and negative:

**Build the system that makes human authority repeatable. Refuse the system that makes human authority synthetic.**

That is the governance principle that should survive every future version of CAE.
