# 7. Subject Constitution, Interview & Structured Elicitation

CAE treats the Leader or Subject as a first-class human authority, not as a file attached to a campaign. This distinction is foundational because the product is ultimately designed to help a recognizable person turn expertise, lived experience, judgment, and communication ability into repeated public activation. A transcript can contain words from that person, but it cannot by itself represent the continuity of the person. The Subject Constitution exists to preserve that continuity while keeping semantic ownership with the human.

The canonical Subject model should therefore contain a persistent identity and a controlled set of baseline attributes. These may include brand context, domain expertise, approved reference material, communication characteristics, selected voice references, deliberately elicited emotional patterns, archetypal hypotheses, audience relationship, historical evidence references, and other fields established by the product contract. Some fields are descriptive. Others are evaluative. Some are stable identifiers. Others are mutable working hypotheses. The system must distinguish these categories because a convenient inference must never silently become an immutable fact about the person.

The most important rule is that the Subject baseline is **elicitation-derived, not arbitrary-data-derived**. The engine must not infer a person's deep psychological constitution from a public social feed, a random transcript, or a pile of marketing copy and then present that inference as canonical identity. The production system should instead provide a dedicated Baseline Elicitation Segment: a structured, Operator-supervised portion of the interview process designed to produce evidence about how the Subject naturally explains ideas, reacts to tension, expresses conviction, tells stories, handles vulnerability, and communicates across different emotional or cognitive contexts. A baseline can be drafted by intelligence, but the human Operator ratifies it.

This creates a useful distinction between **baseline** and **activation**. The baseline is a reference state. Activation often occurs when that state is challenged, contrasted, intensified, clarified, or made relevant to a particular audience. CAE can then reason about the difference between ordinary expression and deliberate variation without confusing variation for inauthenticity. In practical terms, the system may learn that a Subject normally explains complex ideas analytically but becomes unusually vivid when discussing a personal failure. That is useful production knowledge. It should not be converted into a fabricated “personality engine.”

The interview itself is a production instrument governed by the PreProduction Plan. The Operator should not receive a flat list of generic questions. Each question belongs to an elicitation purpose. It may be intended to establish a claim, retrieve a concrete story, surface a contradiction, clarify a mechanism, expose stakes, deepen emotion, obtain an audience-relevant example, or produce a specific form of evidence required by a planned artifact. Questions and follow-ups should therefore be understood as typed tools within an editorial program.

This is why structured elicitation matters. An interview can be conversational and still highly engineered. CAE does not need to turn the Operator into a robotic questionnaire administrator. The plan should give the Operator the destination, the evidence requirement, and the reason a question exists while preserving discretion about conversational delivery. The Operator remains the human conduit capable of reading timing, mood, hesitation, surprise, and trust in ways that are difficult to formalize completely.

Structured elicitation should also recognize that valuable evidence rarely arrives on the first answer. The first answer may be conceptual, defensive, polished, or generic. Follow-up questions can move toward specifics: “What happened?” “What did you actually say?” “When did you realize that?” “What did the other person do?” “What did you believe before?” “What changed?” “What would you never tell a client?” These moves are not content tricks. They are evidence-recovery mechanisms. They turn abstract expertise into source material with enough specificity to survive downstream composition.

CAE can formalize several elicitation states without requiring the machine to conduct the conversation autonomously. A segment can have an objective, a required evidence type, a primary question, follow-up prompts, a desired depth condition, a fallback question, and an exit criterion. This lets the Operator know when a segment has succeeded. Success is not simply “the Subject answered.” Success means the answer generated the kind of evidence the plan declared.

Priming is important here, but it must be framed carefully. Human Operators already use conversational techniques to move a Subject toward a state where deeper expression becomes possible. They may change pacing, introduce contrast, challenge a belief, use humor, touch a point of pride, or ask for a story rather than an opinion. CAE can encode these techniques as structured elicitation guidance and later analyze which forms of questioning produced high-yield evidence. The product should not quietly convert a recommendation into an autonomous real-time psychological intervention. The Operator remains responsible for the live relationship unless a separately ratified product contract explicitly authorizes another mode.

The system should support dual perspectives where useful. A question can be answered as a baseline response and, when deliberately requested by the Operator, as a more audience-activated or persona-driven response. The purpose is not to manufacture two identities. It is to preserve a useful comparison between ordinary communication and communication deliberately shaped for activation. That contrast can help the engine identify what makes the Subject particularly expressive without treating every high-energy performance as the “true” voice.

Evidence quality should be measured during elicitation as well. Strong evidence is usually specific, attributable, consequential, and connected to a recognizable experience or judgment. Weak evidence tends to be abstract, generalized, interchangeable, or unsupported. The Operator should be able to mark an answer as sufficient, insufficient, needing clarification, or requiring a new source. Those dispositions later become valuable production telemetry because they show where the interview protocol succeeded or failed.

The Subject Constitution also needs controlled evolution. A profile should not be overwritten because a model generated a new description. Proposed changes should have provenance and an authority state. A new voice characteristic might be supported by several recent interviews, but it should remain a proposal until accepted through the established governance path. Retired or superseded properties should remain historically traceable rather than disappearing. This permits CAE to distinguish “the Subject once preferred this framing” from “the Subject still prefers this framing.”

This model connects directly to the existing runtime architecture. The repository's Operator runtime already exposes typed actions such as DISCOVER, RUN, INSPECT, PAUSE, RESUME, APPROVE, REJECT, REPAIR, SHIP, and EXPORT_AUDIT, while the runtime models a lossless artifact lineage graph that includes source evidence, evidence segments, semantic annotations, content candidates, storyboards, compositions, rendered artifacts, semantic QA, render QA, and operator approval. That structure is a strong foundation for treating Subject constitution and elicitation as upstream governed objects rather than disposable prompts.

The outcome of structured elicitation is therefore more than a transcript. It is a set of **evidence-bearing expression moments** linked to a Subject, a production plan, a question, a campaign, and the resulting canonical evidence. When those links survive into downstream artifacts, CAE can explain not only where a statement came from but why the interview was designed to obtain it. That is a major part of the product's defensibility.

CAE should consequently define interviewing as a controlled evidence-generation protocol:

**Subject Constitution → Narrative Requirement → Elicitation Segment → Human Conversation → Expression Moment → Canonical Evidence → Artifact.**

This is the opposite of cold-start content automation. The engine does not ask a person to “provide content” and hope useful material appears. It builds a deliberate environment in which the right human evidence is more likely to surface, while preserving the fact that the evidence still came from the human. That is what makes the interview a core product capability rather than a prelude to the “real” AI work.
